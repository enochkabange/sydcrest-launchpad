## Who the track is for and how it differs from neighbouring tracks

The catalogue (`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`, row 17) calls this a new Tier B track:
running pages for businesses (planning, posting, community, basic ads), phone native, with a
Social Media Pages Manager title seen on Jobberman, and flags that it overlaps with Digital
Marketer. Ghana Tech Lab and IPMC run social media courses; Meta runs a Social Media Marketing
certificate on Coursera.

To avoid duplicating existing tracks:

* **Digital Marketer** already owns marketing foundations, copywriting, WhatsApp Business as a
  sales channel, Facebook and Instagram page setup, community management in general, the
  content calendar, paid social and search ads, analytics and client reporting. This track
  points there for real ad campaigns (`boosting-posts`) and does not teach copywriting or ads.
* **Video and Content Creation** owns filming, editing, short video craft, YouTube, building a
  personal audience and copyright. This track points there for production and treats content
  as something a manager plans, gathers and schedules for a client.
* What is left, and what the adverts ask for, is the **operating job of running other people's
  accounts**: onboarding a client, brand voice, safe page access without shared passwords,
  account security, content pillars, gathering content from owners, scheduling with
  approvals, the inbox, moderation and impostor scams, complaints, live selling as a hosted
  service, platform rules, privacy, sponsored post labelling, native insights, monthly reviews,
  page portfolios and managing several clients. Pricing, contracts and getting paid are left to
  the Freelancing track.

* **O*NET Public Relations Specialists (F1, CC BY 4.0):** the closest O*NET occupation; a

  and measuring campaigns. Used only to confirm topic areas; this track deliberately leaves
  the advertising courses to Digital Marketer.

## Outline decisions and why

29 nodes in seven sections.

* **Taking on a client** is its own section because the job is managing someone else's brand:
  onboarding, voice and access come before posting. Page access and account security are
  separate nodes because impostor pages and hijacked accounts are a real risk for Ghanaian
  small businesses and agencies routinely need access to client pages (A3, A4, A8).
* **Planning** stresses pillars, gathering content from owners and approvals, not the calendar
  mechanics that Digital Marketer covers.
* **Live selling** is optional but included because two live streaming roles appeared in one
  October 2026 snapshot (A12, A13), plus a TikTok Live host listing.
* **Boosting** is optional and hands over to Digital Marketer for real ad work.
* **Rules and ethics** covers platform rules, the Data Protection Act and sponsored post labels,
  because influencer partnerships appear in adverts (A14, A15) and customer data lives in
  inboxes.
* **Several clients** points to the Freelancing track for pricing, contracts and payment.

## Lesson wave (issue #198, 27 October 2026)

All 29 nodes now have original lessons, practice tasks and quizzes. No slugs were renamed and
no nodes were added or dropped: the outline already matched the adverts, and the roadmap is
published. Resources were adjusted (at most three per node, each URL returned HTTP 200 on 27

The unused TikTok Help Center and Google 2 Step Verification links were removed.

### How the lessons avoid duplicating neighbouring content

* Digital Marketer already teaches reply etiquette, the basic complaint order, the MoMo PIN
  scam warning, calendar columns, boost versus Ads Manager, live selling as video craft and
  report structure. The lessons here cross reference those and teach the hired manager's
  operating layer instead: agreed reply times and routes, label schemes and an inbox log,
  platform moderation tools and impostor reporting, a three level escalation ladder with a
  first hour checklist, a live selling run sheet with a moderator and order sheet, boosting
  only from the client's own ad account with written spend approval and a spend log, and a
  monthly review that changes next month's pillars.
* The Digital Marketing Specialisations roadmap (social section, separate branch) covers
  campaigns, audiences, budgets, storytelling, video, community building and influencer
  research and collaboration. `honest-promotion` covers only the page manager's side: label
  tools, approving partner requests, checking claims and FDA approval, and a written brief.
