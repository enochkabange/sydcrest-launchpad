# Research: Excel and Power BI track (`excel-power-bi`)

Tier B outline (issue #96). Researched and written 27 October 2026. Outline only: lessons are
marked "coming soon" by the site. Everything in `src/content/roadmaps/excel-power-bi.ts` is
original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap" style searches
were run. No logins; LinkedIn was not opened. Job adverts were read for which skills appear; no
advert text is reproduced. Salary figures were ignored.

## Who the track is for

Excel reporting is asked for across accounting, admin, MIS, monitoring and evaluation, and
business intelligence roles; Power BI appears increasingly alongside it. The catalogue
(`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`) lists Excel and Power BI courses at Blossom, IPMC, Moringa,
Microsoft and others. This track is a short, practical office data skill and a step before the
full `data` (Data Analyst) track, which goes on to SQL and Python. Field category `data` was
chosen because the work is reporting and analysis, even though many of the jobs sit in finance
and admin.

Phone first: Excel for the web and the Excel mobile app (free with a Microsoft account, with
some limits) and Google Sheets cover the first four sections. Power BI Desktop runs only on
Windows, so the Power BI section needs a Windows laptop, for example at a library, school lab
or internet café; the outline says so at that node. The Power BI mobile app can view reports.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **O*NET Business Intelligence Analysts (F1, CC BY 4.0):** producing standard and custom reports,
  maintaining dashboards, supporting report users, spotting trends. Tools: Power BI, Excel, SQL
  reporting tools. Skills: critical thinking, mathematics, writing, systems analysis.
* **Power BI Data Analyst Associate, PL 300 (C1):** prepare the data, model the data, visualise and
  analyse, manage and secure; proficiency in Power Query and DAX. Confirms the Power BI section
  order of get data, model, measure, report, share.
* **MOS Excel Associate, MO 200 (C2):** worksheets, cells and ranges, tables, formulas and
  functions, charts. Confirms the Excel sections. Named in learner copy only as an option, without
  a cost claim or endorsement.

## Outline decisions and why

27 nodes in seven sections.

* **Excel first, Power BI second**: every Power BI advert also expects Excel, and Excel works on a
  phone.
* **Lookups and PivotTables get their own nodes**: the two most named Excel features (A3, A4).
* **Cleaning and validation** is a node because three of the four full listings mention data
  quality.
* **Power Query appears twice by design**: once in Excel (repeatable cleaning) and once as the way
  data enters Power BI, since they share the same engine (C4).
* **Sharing and refresh** warns that "publish to web" makes a report public, a real data
  protection risk for NGO and bank data.
* **Laptop honesty**: the Power BI section summary says a Windows laptop is needed.
