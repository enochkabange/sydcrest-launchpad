# Research: Cybersecurity Analyst track (`cybersecurity-analyst`)

Wave 3. Researched and written 25 October 2026. Everything in the outline and lesson content
is original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap" style
searches were run. No logins; LinkedIn was not scraped (LinkedIn profile URLs appeared in
search results and were ignored). Job adverts were read for which skills appear; no advert
text is reproduced here or in the content.

## Who the track is for

Learners who have finished, or are taking, SydCrest's IT support track. The adverts below
show that junior security work in Ghana sits mostly in telcos, banks and savings and loans
companies, fintechs and audit firms, almost all in Accra, with some IT officer roles in
regional banks that carry security duties. Truly entry level security adverts are rare in
Ghana: most "analyst" adverts ask for experience, and the ones that welcome graduates are
national service and internship posts (audit firms' technology risk teams) or SOC talent
pools abroad. Remote Level 1 SOC roles exist but are often restricted to one country. That
is why the track treats IT support, help desk, fraud monitoring and national service as the
realistic doors, and says so in the lessons.

The track catalogue (Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md) notes cybersecurity programmes from
Ghana Digital Centres with YEA (press), IPMC, Moringa, Google, IBM and EC Council, and rates
the track "fair" for phones because real roles need IT support first. The content says
honestly that labs need a computer and offers phone alternatives.

## Framework skills

* **O*NET 15-1212.00 Information Security Analysts (F1):** tasks include safeguarding files
  against unauthorised change or disclosure, monitoring virus reports, encrypting data and
  erecting firewalls, risk assessments and system tests, modifying access status. Technology
  skills include access management software, network monitoring (Wireshark named), operating
  systems (Bash, Windows Server, Linux), network security tools, and Splunk. Top skills are
  reading comprehension, critical thinking, active listening, speaking and writing, which
  supports the track's emphasis on clear ticket notes and awareness talks.
* **ESCO (F2):** unavailable on the day read.
* **Course topic lists (C1 to C3), names only:** security foundations and risk; networks;
  Linux and SQL; assets, threats and vulnerabilities; detection and response with SIEM;
  Python automation; job preparation (Google). ISC2 CC domains: security principles,
  security governance, IAM concepts, networking and cloud security concepts, security
  operations and incident response. These confirmed the outline's spread, not its order.

## Ghana specific facts used (all from official or primary sources)

* Act 1038: CSA established (s.2); CII designation and duties (s.35 to 40); National and
  Sectoral CERTs (s.41 to 46); duty to report: a person in charge of an institution reports to
  the Sectoral or National CERT within 24 hours of detection (s.47(5)); licensing of
  cybersecurity service providers (s.49); accreditation of professionals and practitioners
  (s.57); public awareness (s.60); child online protection and sexual extortion (s.62 to 66);
  non consensual sharing of intimate images (s.67); s.94 covers unlawful retrieval of
  subscriber information or interception, and is not described as a general hacking offence.
* Act 772 s.124: intentionally accessing or intercepting an electronic record without
  authority or permission is an offence. s.118 was repealed by Act 1038 s.98, so it is not
  cited. No penalty figures are used.
* CSA licensing began 1 March 2023 in five areas (VAPT, digital forensics, managed
  cybersecurity services, GRC, training). The lesson tells learners to check current rules
  before offering services to clients, and does not say accreditation is needed for an in
  house job.
* Reporting channels from csa.gov.gh/report only.
* BoG October 2026 notice: no legitimate provider asks for PINs, passwords or OTPs; avoid
  double your money schemes; report SIM swap incidents to providers.
* CERT GH May 2024 alert: WhatsApp takeovers via fake admin or trusted contact pretexts, with
  some cases escalating to SIM swap.
* Statistics seen in news (SIM swap loss totals, case counts, workforce shortage claims) are
  deliberately not used.

## Changes made from the first outline and why

There was no prior outline (new wave 3 track). Decisions made while drafting against the
adverts:

* **Added `frameworks-standards`.** ISO 27001 appeared in six adverts and PCI DSS and the BoG
  directive in several bank and audit adverts, which a pure skills outline would have missed.
* **Merged "threats, vulnerabilities and risk" into `cia-and-risk`** to keep the foundations
  section short and the node count in the mid twenties (27 nodes).
* **Made `entry-certifications` optional.** Certifications appear in adverts, but none of the
  junior or graduate adverts required one, and cost is a real barrier.
* **Fraud focus in `momo-fraud-sim-swap`.** Telco and fintech employers plus the BoG notice
  make local fraud patterns a practical differentiator.
* **Gaps not given their own node:** scripting (Python, Bash, PowerShell) appeared only in two
  adverts, both asking two or more years' experience, so it is left as a later step rather
  than a node; cloud security (two adverts) is mentioned inside other lessons; penetration
  testing (one Ghanaian advert) is deliberately excluded because the track is defensive and
  such services are licensed by the CSA.
