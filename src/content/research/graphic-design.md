# Research: Graphic Designer track (`graphic-designer`)

Wave 3 (new track). Researched and written 25 October 2026. Everything in the outline and lesson
content is original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap" style
searches were run. No logins; LinkedIn was not scraped. Job adverts were read for which skills
appear; no advert text is reproduced here or in the content. Salary figures seen on some adverts
are deliberately left out of this file and the lessons.

## Who the track is for

Graphic design is one of the most common course names across Ghanaian training providers (ALX,
OpenLabs, IPMC and Ghana Tech Lab all list it in `Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`), mostly as
classroom cohorts in Accra and other big cities. SydCrest's version is the free, phone first
on ramp: the lessons say plainly that learners can use it on its own or to prepare for a cohort.

The adverts show four doors into paid design work: print shops and branding or souvenir companies
(print production, CorelDRAW, fast flyers and banners), agencies (campaigns, motion, portfolio),
in house marketing teams (brand guidelines, social and marketing collateral) and NGOs or schools
(educational materials, reports, certificates). Remote roles exist but usually ask for experience,
a laptop, stable internet and sometimes residence in a named country.

**No design adverts in Tamale, Bolgatanga or Wa surfaced** on Jobberman, BusinessGhana or the
search engines on the day (searches recorded below). Every Jobberman Creative and Design listing
in the snapshot was in Accra and Tema; the only non Accra signals were two Kumasi classified ads and
one remote, home based Ghana NGO role. Northern demand therefore shows up as print shops, churches,
schools, NGOs, events and freelance work rather than board postings, and the `freelancing` and
`job-search` lessons say this honestly without promising outcomes.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **O*NET Graphic Designers (F1, CC BY 4.0):** tasks include creating and revising layouts, choosing type and images, designing logos and illustrations, preparing digital files for print, marking up layouts for printers, consulting clients, researching audiences and creating animated graphics. Technology list includes Adobe tools, Canva and Figma. Skills include active listening, critical thinking, time management, negotiation and persuasion; knowledge areas include design, communications and media, sales and marketing, customer service and production. Used to justify the client work and pricing nodes (client consultation, negotiation, sales, customer service), which adverts imply but rarely list.
* **ESCO (F2):** unavailable on the day; not used.
* **GCFGlobal Beginning Graphic Design (C1):** topic names confirm that typography, colour, layout and composition, images, design fundamentals and branding are the standard beginner topics. No text or ordering taken; SydCrest's order follows the adverts (print and social work first, then client and career skills).
* **Official docs (D1 to D7):** confirmed Inkscape is desktop only and open source; the WCAG 4.5 to 1 contrast guidance; Creative Commons licence codes; that the Open Font License allows commercial use; that Ghanaian copyright does not depend on registration and that the Registrar General registers trademarks.

## Outline design and why

New track, 25 nodes in six sections (19 topics plus 6 section nodes), one optional.

* **Foundations first, phone first.** `design-tools` teaches raster versus vector and names free tools only as examples (Canva, Photopea, GIMP, Inkscape). It says plainly that Inkscape and precise vector or print work need a laptop and points to school labs, libraries and community centres without naming any.
* **Print gets its own node (`print-production`).** Print materials appear in 9 of 20 adverts and print file preparation, CorelDRAW and colour separation recur in print shop adverts. The lesson teaches bleed, safe area, 300 ppi, CMYK, PDF export and outlining fonts generically, and tells learners to ask the printer, because sources conflict on specific tools' free plan features (D8).
* **CorelDRAW is mentioned, not taught.** It is paid; the lessons teach the same concepts in free tools and note that print shops often want PDFs with fonts converted to outlines ("curves").
* **Branding split into `logo-design` and `brand-identity`.** Brand guidelines appear in 7 adverts and logos in 5.
* **`motion-basics` is optional.** Five adverts mention animation, mostly agency or senior roles.
* **Multi page layout folded into `flyers-and-posters`** (programmes, booklets in multiples of four, Scribus for long documents) rather than a separate node, keeping the outline at 25.
* **Client work section (`briefs-and-feedback`, `copyright-and-ethics`, `pricing-and-invoicing`).** Briefs and revisions appear in 5 adverts; pricing and invoicing in cedis is in the founder's scope for this track and justified by O*NET client consultation, negotiation and sales knowledge, plus the freelance route that northern learners rely on. AI image tools are folded into `copyright-and-ethics` (one advert, A7).
* **No salary or price claims.** The pricing lesson uses an explicitly made up example calculation and tells learners to ask local designers and print shops.
