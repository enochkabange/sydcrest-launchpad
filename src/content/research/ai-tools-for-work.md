# Research: AI Tools for Work track (`ai-tools-for-work`)

Wave 3. Researched and written 25 October 2026. Everything in the outline and lesson
content is original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap"
style searches were run. No logins; LinkedIn was not scraped. Job adverts were read for
which skills appear; no advert text is reproduced here or in the content. Pay figures seen
in adverts are deliberately left out of this file and the lessons.

## Who the track is for

"AI tools for work" is not a job title. It is a layer of skill that adverts now add to
familiar junior roles: virtual and admin assistants, content and communications staff,
social media and creative roles, junior automation roles, and remote AI data work
(transcription, translation and rating AI answers) that recruits native speakers of
Ghanaian languages. Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md ranks it the top Tier A upgrade because
every local competitor leads with AI and it runs fully on a phone. Many local AI skills
programmes are cohort based in Accra; the portfolio lesson says honestly that learners can
use this roadmap to prepare, without naming any programme as a partner.

Ghana specific adverts that name AI tools were scarcer than remote ones on the day read.
Ghanaian job market commentary (C1 to C3) says AI skills are becoming an advantage and that
employers still value human judgement most. The track is built around that balance:
"AI drafts, you decide".

Patterns: (1) AI is an add on to writing, admin and creative skill, never a replacement;
(2) accuracy and checking AI output recur in every content advert; (3) remote AI data work

social media adverts did not mention AI at all, which supports teaching AI as a layer over
other tracks rather than a standalone career.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **ESCO (F1):** ESCO's own study of what people ask ChatGPT to do maps mostly to
  summarisation, content creation and problem solving. Supports the everyday work section.
  The ESCO skill search API was unavailable (F2).
* **O*NET (F3, F4):** ChatGPT appears only under software and data occupations, so O*NET
  does not yet describe office AI use. The administrative assistant profile's task themes
  (correspondence, reports, scheduling, data entry, research) are exactly the tasks the
  everyday work and automation sections apply AI to.
* **UNESCO AI competency framework for students (F5):** four dimensions (a human centred
  mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, AI system design). The first three
  shape the basics, privacy, ethics and "you are responsible" threads. AI system design is
  out of scope for a phone first beginner track.
* **DigComp 2.2 (F6):** adds examples about interacting with AI systems across information
  literacy, content creation and safety. Supports checking facts, privacy and images.
* **Course topic lists (K1, K2):** intro to AI, productivity, prompting, responsible use and
  staying current. Topic names only; neither text nor ordering was used.

## Outline changes and why

There was no earlier outline; this is a new wave 3 track. My first draft, written from the
brief's scope before reading adverts, was revised as follows.

* **Fact checking promoted to its own node** (`checking-facts`) inside the prompting section,
  because checking AI output appears in every content advert (A1 A2 A16 A17).
* **AI data work and scam safety added** to `ai-jobs-gigs`, because remote transcription and
  AI training adverts for Twi, Akan and Dagbani (A7 to A10) are a concrete opportunity for
  northern learners, and such work attracts fake "registration fee" offers.
* **Translation and local languages kept as a full node,** strengthened by A7 to A11 and C4.
* **Automation split into two nodes** (`prompt-templates`, `automating-tasks`): A13 to A15
  ask for no code tools, but those are easiest on a computer, so the lesson starts with phone
  features (quick replies, text shortcuts, form to sheet) and says plainly where a computer
  helps.
* **Images marked optional:** only creative adverts (A4 to A6) ask for AI image or video
  skills, and image generation is data heavy on a prepaid bundle.
* **Digital products and selling split into two nodes,** tying to SydCrest's September 2026
  event (P1). The product lesson says plainly that coded AI apps need a laptop and points to
  the developer tracks.
* **Dropped a planned "AI for coding" node:** it needs a laptop and belongs in the developer
  tracks.
* **Portfolio capstone added** (`ai-portfolio`): adverts ask for "shown" or "demonstrated"
  AI use (A3 A12), which only evidence can satisfy.

Skills adverts asked for that this outline only touches: design tools, social media
scheduling and SEO (A2 A4 A19 A20) are covered in depth by the digital marketing track and
the planned graphic design track, so this track links to them in spirit rather than
duplicating them.
