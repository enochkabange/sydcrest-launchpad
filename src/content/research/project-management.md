## Who the track is for

In northern Ghana, project work is mostly NGO and donor funded: project assistants, project
officers, programme support officers and coordinators at international NGOs, local NGOs, UN
agencies and hubs in Tamale, Bolgatanga and Wa. Businesses hire project coordinators and
assistants too, especially in construction, real estate and events. The catalogue
(`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`) notes Google and Microsoft project management certificates
and a Jobberman Product and Project Management category, and positions this as a good second
step rather than a first job for most learners. The outline therefore aims at assistant, intern
and national service roles first, and frames officer and coordinator roles as goals.

Field category `business` (best registered fit; `product` is used for the separate Product
Manager track).

## Frameworks and curricula

* **O*NET Project Management Specialists (F1, CC BY 4.0):** stakeholder requirements, planning
  and scheduling, resources and budgets, monitoring costs and milestones, vendors, status
  reports, problem solving. Tools: Excel, Google Workspace, Jira, Asana, Teams.
* **Google Project Management certificate (C1):** course titles: foundations, initiation,
  planning, execution, agile, capstone. Confirms the life cycle structure. The page says it
  helps prepare for CAPM.
* **Project DPro (C2, CC BY NC 4.0):** a free guide written for development sector project
  managers, adopted as a standard by many development organisations. Used to justify the NGO
  focus (logframes, donor reporting) and named as a factual option.
* **CAPM (C3):** PMI's entry certification; eligibility seen on pmi.org snippets is a secondary
  credential plus 23 hours of project management education. Learner copy names it without
  detail or cost and tells learners to check the official page.
* **PRINCE2 Foundation (C4)** and the **Scrum Guide (C5)**: named as other recognised entry
  points; no endorsement.

## Outline decisions and why

27 nodes in seven sections.

* **Budgets get their own node and appear in tracking**: budget and financial tracking is the
  most common signal, in every full listing.
* **Logframes** are a node because NGO roles in the north work to donor results frameworks (A3,
  A4, A9, A11; C2), even when adverts do not say the word.
* **Logistics and procurement** is a node because assistant roles (A1) spend much of their time on
  it.
* **Task boards** use Trello or a spreadsheet as examples so learners can practise free on a phone.
* **Agile is optional**: no advert asked for it, but C1 teaches it and some business roles use it.
* **Certifications are named factually** in one node (CAPM, Project DPro, PRINCE2 Foundation,
  Google certificate) with no endorsement and no cost claims.
