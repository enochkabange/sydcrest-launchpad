# Research: No Code and Automation track (`no-code`)

Tier B outline (issue #96), researched and written 27 October 2026. Original lessons and quizzes
for every node were added on 28 October 2026 (issue #198) in
`src/content/roadmaps/content/no-code.content.ts`; the outline lives in
`src/content/roadmaps/no-code.ts`. Everything is original. roadmap.sh was not opened,
searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap" style searches were run. No logins; LinkedIn was
not opened. Job adverts were read only for which skills and tools appear; no advert text is
reproduced. Salary and price figures that appeared in some results were ignored and are not
recorded or used.

## Who the track is for

No code work in Ghana rarely carries its own job title. The catalogue
(`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`) found no Jobberman title for it, and this research found the
same. The skills instead sit inside other roles: enumerators and data collection officers who
use mobile survey tools (KoboToolbox, ODK, SurveyCTO), M&E staff who turn that data into Excel
or Power BI dashboards, communications and IT officers who maintain WordPress sites, and remote
operations roles that build and monitor Zapier, Make or Airtable workflows. Freelance demand
also shows up as small client projects (forms, sheet based apps, automations). The track is
therefore framed as a set of practical building skills that support data collection,
operations, website and freelance work, and the outline does not promise a "no code
developer" job.

## Phone versus laptop (honest notes used in summaries)

* Phone works well: filling and building simple online forms, collecting survey data in
  offline Android apps (KoboCollect, ODK Collect), checking spreadsheets, using finished apps,
  reviewing automation run history, small website edits.
* Laptop or cafe computer strongly preferred: designing XLSForm surveys, building sheet based
  apps, WordPress setup and themes, building multi step automations. Vendor editors for app
  builders and automation tools are designed for desktop browsers.
* Free plans: every tool family named has a free tier with limits on records, users or task
  runs, and those limits change, so the outline says to read the plan page rather than
  stating limits or prices.

## Outline decisions and why

28 nodes, six sections. Tools are named only as examples, with at least two per family where
possible, and none is recommended over another.

* **Data first section before apps and automation** because every app builder and automation
  in the adverts runs on a table (A10 to A15), and dashboards appear in M&E roles (A4, A5).
* **Field surveys get two nodes** (`field-surveys`, optional `survey-logic`) because mobile data
  collection is the strongest Ghanaian signal (five adverts, including Tamale) and fits phones.
* **WordPress is a full node** inside websites (four adverts, O*NET tool list, catalogue row 27).
  Hosting, domains, speed and accessibility are merged into `going-live` to stay within 30 nodes.
* **AI steps are optional** (`ai-steps`): one advert (A11) and the catalogue mention agents, but
  the tools change quickly. The summary stresses human checking and privacy, and links to the
  separate AI Tools for Work track topics are left to the lesson writer.
* **Privacy is its own node** because forms and surveys collect personal data, and Ghana's
  Data Protection Act applies (D8).
* **Earning is folded into one section with privacy and handover**, and points learners at the
  roles where these skills appear (data collection, operations, website and communications
  work, freelance projects) rather than promising a no code job title. Pricing is mentioned
  without figures; the Freelancing track covers it in depth.

## Lesson writing (28 October 2026, issue #198)

* **Coverage:** all 28 nodes, sections included, have an original explanation (499 to 599
  words), a practice task doable with free or already owned tools and little data, and a 4 or 5
  question quiz. Correct answers are spread across positions (29/28/31/32 over 120 questions)
  and the correct option is the longest in under a fifth of questions.
* **Slugs:** unchanged (the roadmap is published). `phone-or-laptop` keeps its slug; only its
  title changed to "Phone, computer and data honestly".
* **Outline wording:** summaries that said "laptop" now say "computer" (device neutral rule);
  no nodes added, dropped or reordered, because the existing research already justified every
  node and the lesson writing found no gap.
* **Facts checked on official pages (D10 to D22):** each device claim (Android only collection
  apps, Kobo web forms on any device, Sites setup on a computer), Forms branching question types,
  WordPress roles, AppSheet concepts, Zapier and Make terms, WhatsApp Business greeting, away
  and quick replies, and the DPC principles and registration line.
* **Not stated, on purpose:** no prices and no free plan limits for any tool. The KoboToolbox
  pricing page returned 403 to automated reads, so its plans are not described at all; Glide
  publishing terms were not described. Lessons tell learners to read and date each plans page.
* **No job title:** lessons frame no code as skills inside data collection, M&E,
  communications, operations and freelance work, never as a "no code developer" job.
* **Data protection:** Act 843 is introduced in `forms`, `online-forms` and `field-surveys`,
  taught in `privacy-and-permissions`, and applied to AI steps in `ai-steps`. Lessons say they
  are general guidance, not legal advice.
