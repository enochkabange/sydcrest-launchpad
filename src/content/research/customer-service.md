## Who the track is for

Entry customer service work in Ghana sits under titles such as customer service
representative, call centre agent, customer service agent, live chat agent, customer
experience associate, lead caller and front desk executive. Employers seen: banks (through
recruiters), logistics and delivery firms, fintechs, a web hosting company, an EV charging
company, retail and hospitality, and remote support firms serving overseas clients. The track
catalogue (`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`) lists Generation Ghana's free 8 week online
Digital Customer Service programme as the main local training route and Customer Service and
Support as a live Jobberman category. The outline is designed so a learner can prepare for
programmes like that and for entry roles directly, on a phone or a computer.

Field category `business` (best registered fit: support work sits in operations and customer
experience teams rather than software, marketing or data).

## Frameworks and curricula

* **O*NET Customer Service Representatives (F1, CC BY 4.0):** tasks cover answering enquiries,
  resolving complaints (refunds, adjustments), keeping records of interactions, handling account
  changes, referring customers to other departments and suggesting process improvements. Tools
  include office suites, CRM, contact centre and help desk software. Skills: active listening,
  speaking, writing, service orientation, problem solving, time management, social
  perceptiveness.
* **Generation Ghana Digital Customer Service (C1):** keywords on the page: issue identification
  and resolution, customer etiquette, product knowledge, multitasking, typing speed, sales
  tactics, stress management, first level technical troubleshooting. Eight weeks, online, free;
  eligibility includes ages 18 to 35, Greater Accra, secondary certificate or higher, day and
  night shift availability. Used to confirm topic areas only; the outline tells learners to
  check eligibility on the official page and does not state it as fixed.

## Outline decisions and why

26 nodes in six sections.

* **Channels get their own section** (phone, chat and WhatsApp, email, social) because adverts
  split evenly between phone and written support, and A3 names WhatsApp directly.
* **Tools section** covers tickets, CRM records and knowledge bases: help desk and CRM tools are
  the single most common named skill. Zendesk, Freshdesk and Zoho Desk are named only as
  examples with free plans or trials, not endorsed.
* **Payments and account security** is a node of its own because banking, betting and fintech
  employers dominate, and mobile money fraud is a real risk for customers and agents.
* **Local languages** sit in the foundations section because A10 asks for one and northern
  learners often speak Dagbani, Hausa or another language alongside English.
* **Stress and shift work** is included because Generation teaches stress management and three
  adverts are shift or remote roles.
* Overlap with the virtual assistant track (open PR #99) is kept small: this track is about
  support queues, tickets and service quality, not inbox and calendar admin.

## Lesson writing notes (issue #198)

* Every explanation, practice task, script, scenario and quiz is original. Businesses in
  examples (Kpalang Parcels and the unnamed delivery, savings, school fees, internet and insurance
  firms) are invented. Names follow the content guide's Northern Ghanaian list.
* Mobile money is described without naming any provider. The Bank of Ghana, the Cyber
  Security Authority and the Data Protection Commission are named as official bodies, not
  endorsed products. The only law cited is the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843).
* Zendesk, Freshdesk, Zoho Desk and WhatsApp Business appear only as examples of tool types.
  Learner copy names no training programme: after review, the founder chose a generic mention
  of free programmes run by employers and nonprofits, with a prompt to check location and
  eligibility, because Generation Ghana (C1) only accepts Greater Accra residents.
* No statistics, salary figures or response time targets are given; where an example uses a
  time (such as "by 4pm"), it is a promise made inside an invented scenario.
* Practice tasks use invented customer data only and need just a notes app, a free
  spreadsheet, a voice recorder and a browser.

## Review notes (independent content review, 27 October 2026)

* Act 843 is still the law in force; a Data Protection Bill, 2025 proposing to repeal it had not
  passed when checked (D2), so the data lesson now tells learners to check the Commission's site.
* Generation Ghana's programme page (C1) still lists eligibility limited to Greater Accra and
  partly dated intake text, so the lesson now tells learners to check location as well.
* WhatsApp Business features named in the lessons (business profile, greeting and away
  messages, quick replies, labels) were checked against W1 and W2.
