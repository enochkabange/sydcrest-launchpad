# Research: IT Support and Networking track (`it-support`)

Wave 3. Researched and written 25 October 2026. This is a new track: there was no draft
outline, so the outline in `src/content/roadmaps/it-support.ts` was designed from this research.
Everything in the outline and lesson content is original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched
or used, and no "<field> roadmap" style searches were run. No logins; LinkedIn was not opened
or scraped (one LinkedIn post appeared only inside a search summary and is not counted). Job
adverts were read for which skills appear; no advert text is reproduced here or in the content.
Salary figures that appeared on some job pages were ignored and are not recorded.

## Who the track is for

Junior IT support work in Ghana sits under many titles: IT support officer, IT assistant,
help desk or service desk analyst, desktop support technician or engineer, ICT officer, lab
technician, NOC support engineer. Employers seen: banks, community and rural banks and
microfinance companies; international NGOs and UN agencies (including one role in Tamale);
universities; a telco; manufacturers; print service companies; and IT service firms that
supply desktop support to other businesses. Most listings are in Accra and Tema, with Kumasi,
Takoradi, Obuasi and Tamale also appearing. The catalogue (`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`)
already flagged this track as the natural gateway into cybersecurity and cloud, and found
IT support programmes at OpenLabs, IPMC (CompTIA N+ short course), AmaliTech (AWS re/Start),
Google and AWS. Many of those are cohort or campus based, so the track tells learners
honestly that they can use it to prepare.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **O*NET Computer User Support Specialists (F1, CC BY 4.0):** tasks include setting up equipment
  and software, answering user questions, diagnosing problems with diagnostics and manuals,
  installing hardware and software, minor repairs, training users. Tool groups include help desk
  software, Active Directory, remote access, network monitoring, antivirus and backup. Skills:
  active listening, troubleshooting, service orientation, instructing, writing.
* **O*NET Computer Network Support Specialists (F2, CC BY 4.0):** backing up network data,
  configuring permissions, diagnosing network problems, configuring routers and wireless
  equipment, documenting support, training users. Tools include Windows Server, Linux and Bash,
  Active Directory, ServiceNow.
* **ESCO (F3):** unavailable on the day (redirected to the service unavailable page); not used.
* **Google IT Support Professional Certificate (C1):** course titles seen: technical support
  fundamentals; computer networking; operating systems; system administration and IT
  infrastructure; IT security; job search. Skill tags include customer support, documentation,
  hardware, Linux and Windows, TCP/IP, DHCP, DNS related networking, Active Directory, backups and
  disaster recovery. Used only to confirm the topic areas a beginner IT support track covers; no
  text or ordering taken.
* **CompTIA A+ (C2):** two exams, Core 1 hardware and networking, Core 2 operating systems and
  security. Confirms the hardware, networking, OS and security split of the outline.
* **Local qualifications (P1, D10, D11):** the catalogue lists OpenLabs (Network Engineering,
  CCNA, Hardware and Networking, campuses reported including Tamale) and IPMC (CompTIA N+ short
  course, System Engineering diploma); a 2015 report describes NVTI accreditation of a private
  provider's computer hardware and networking courses; CTVET describes a national competency
  based qualifications framework. Adverts accepted HND or diploma in some bank roles (S21, a
  search summary of a rural bank advert) and a post secondary certificate in A5. Content names
  these routes generically and tells learners to check accreditation on official pages.

## Outline decisions and why

New outline, 26 nodes in six sections. Decisions driven by the research:

* **Hardware gets its own section** (`computer-components`, `maintenance-and-upgrades`,
  `power-protection`, `printers-and-peripherals`) because troubleshooting, installation,
  maintenance and inventory are the most common signals (A1, A3, A5, A7, A19) and printers have
  roles of their own (A17, A18) plus first line mentions (A13, A19).
* **Accounts and directory got a dedicated node** (`accounts-and-access`) rather than being a
  line inside security. Active Directory, Microsoft 365, Entra and Intune appear in six adverts,
  more than the scope list implied.
* **Tickets and SLAs moved into the first section** (`tickets-and-customer-care`), next to the
  troubleshooting method, because six adverts name ticketing, SLAs or escalation and almost all
  name communication. ServiceNow, Zendesk and Jira are mentioned as examples only; the catalogue
  says vendor platforms like ServiceNow are better as mentions than nodes.
* **Remote support and mobile device management** (A14, A15, A20) were folded into
  `windows-support` (Quick Assist) and `android-and-mobile` (MDM), not separate nodes.
* **Linux is a full node** (`linux-basics`) despite one advert naming it, because O*NET F2, the
  Google certificate and both follow on paths (cybersecurity, cloud) require it, and A2 shows it
  in a Ghana NOC role.
* **Networking has four topics** (`ip-addresses`, `dns`, `wifi-and-routers`,
  `network-troubleshooting`) because networking is the second most common signal and the
  certifications adverts prefer (Network+, CCNA) are networking ones.
* **Power protection** is included without an advert signal, as a Ghana specific prerequisite of
  `maintenance-and-upgrades` and hardware longevity; it is one node, not a section.
* **Certifications and local diplomas** are one node (`qualifications`), naming certifications
  that appear in adverts (A+, Network+, CCNA, ITIL, Microsoft) and the Google certificate, factually
  and without endorsement. MCSA is mentioned only to say it was retired (D8), because A7 still asks
  for it.
* **Next steps** (`next-steps`) leads into cybersecurity, network engineering, systems
  administration and cloud, matching the catalogue's view of IT support as the gateway track.
* **Not included as nodes:** CCTV (A7, one advert), VMware and Veeam (A9), core banking systems
  (A8), monitoring stacks such as Grafana or Zabbix (A2). These are employer specific or second
  line; virtualisation and monitoring are mentioned in `next-steps` and `networking`.
