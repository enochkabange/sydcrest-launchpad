# Research: Virtual Assistant and Remote Work track

Track file: `src/content/roadmaps/virtual-assistant.ts` (roadmap slug `virtual-assistant`, new in wave 3).
Lesson content: `src/content/roadmaps/content/virtual-assistant.content.ts`.
Researched 25 October 2026 for issue #96 (wave 3), following the issue #84 process.

## Method and provenance

* Job adverts were read for which skills, tools and working conditions appear. No advert text is copied anywhere in the roadmap or lessons; the tables below record keywords only.
* Every individual Jobberman listing URL we opened redirected to the Admin and Office category page, so local adverts were read either as listing cards on that category page (C) or through their search result snippet (S). Full advert text (F) was not available for any Jobberman role.
* Two remote advert pages returned 404 or a certificate error when fetched; those are used only through search snippets and marked S.
* No roadmap.sh pages, no "field roadmap" searches, no logins, no LinkedIn pages. Freelancer profile pages (Hubstaff Talent, Upwork "hire" pages) appeared in results; they are candidate profiles, not adverts, and were not counted. One Accra freelancer profile list was noted only as a signal that local VAs offer social media scheduling and Canva work.
* Pay figures shown on some adverts and pages were deliberately not recorded. Vendor written guides on international payment fees were read but their percentages are not used anywhere (unverified, and the guide forbids unsourced figures).
* Some local adverts contained gender or age requirements; those requirements were not recorded and are not reflected in the track.

### Key finding on eligibility

Most remote assistant adverts found were restricted to named countries (South Africa, Ethiopia, South Sudan, India, Costa Rica). Only advert 1 was explicitly worldwide, and the "Remote from Anywhere" tags in advert 19 could not be confirmed. Remote adverts also specified a reliable laptop and fast, stable internet, and several were independent contractor roles on a foreign client's hours. This shaped the `finding-clients` lesson (check location, hours, equipment and payment before applying), the `working-reliably` section (honest about equipment, with phone and shared computer fallbacks) and the `getting-work` section (local work as an equally valid first route).

## Framework skills

* **O*NET Secretaries and Administrative Assistants (43-6014.00):** manage email, schedule appointments and calendars, draft correspondence and reports, maintain paper and electronic files, enter data, research information online, proofread, coordinate meetings and events, arrange travel, answer phones and greet visitors. Skills: active listening, writing, reading comprehension, time management, service orientation. Tools: Microsoft Office and Google Workspace, Outlook, Google Drive, Slack, Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Canva, document management.
* **O*NET Executive Secretaries and Executive Administrative Assistants (43-6011.00):** manage executive schedules, arrange travel, prepare invoices, reports and memos, prepare meeting agendas and minutes, research and compile data, file and retrieve records, set up procedures. Tools add Airtable, Evernote, OneNote, Dropbox, scheduling software, accounting software.
* **O*NET Customer Service Representatives (43-4051.00):** handle enquiries, resolve complaints, process orders and refunds, keep records, refer to other departments. Skills: active listening, service orientation, problem solving, social perceptiveness. Tools: CRM, Microsoft Office, Slack, Google Docs, Zoom.
* **ESCO:** unavailable on the research date (API network error), so not used.
* **Track catalogue:** VA ranked as a Pick, phone friendly and able to earn in foreign currency; digital customer service and freelancing are to be folded into VA as modules for now. ALX runs VA as a standalone programme, so the `getting-work` section says honestly that this roadmap can be used to prepare for cohort programmes, without naming any as a partner.

## Gaps found and what changed

There was no earlier outline for this track, so the outline was written from the research. Decisions:

* **Events and travel** appear in advert 5 and both O*NET admin occupations but in no entry level remote advert, so they are folded into `calendar-management` rather than given a node.
* **File organisation and sharing** were folded into `documents` (renamed "Documents, templates and shared files") to keep the track at 26 nodes.
* **Agreements, rates and invoices** were folded into `getting-paid`, since clients rarely separate them from payment in practice.
* **Customer support** gets one node, not a section: the catalogue lists digital customer service as its own Tier B track, so this node teaches only the basics a VA needs.
* **Social media scheduling** is kept as a required node (advert and profile signals), scoped to scheduling and reporting; strategy and paid ads are left to the Digital Marketer track.
* **Bookkeeping tools (QuickBooks)** appeared in advert 2 and O*NET but not in entry level Ghanaian adverts, so they are not covered.
* **AI tools** were not given a node (an AI tools for work track exists in wave 3); they appear only as cautions in `professional-writing`, `online-research` and `account-security`.
* **Eligibility and equipment** turned out to be the most important honest message for Ghana based learners and are taught in `finding-clients` and `working-reliably`.
