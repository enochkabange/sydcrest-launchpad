## Demand: an honest note

The catalogue (`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`, row 23) found no surveyed platform offering
software testing as a programme and no Ghana software testing title on Jobberman on the day it
was read; Jobberman's quality category was mostly manufacturing. It kept the track at Tier B
because testing is beginner friendly and partly phone based.

This round of research found more than the catalogue did, but still not much: a handful of
Accra testing adverts at fintech, agritech, mobile engagement and product companies (A1 to A8),
several of them closed or from 2022 and 2023, and only one or two clearly entry level (A1, A4).
Remote junior roles seen were tied to other countries (A11, A12). There is no basis here for
any claim about how many testing jobs exist. The track description and `what-testers-do` say
plainly that only a handful of openings were found, mostly in Accra, and the outline
points toward automation, remote work and adjacent roles so the skills stay useful.

testing, mobile testing and automation are the commonest asks. Two things went into lessons

Remote roles open to Ghana based applicants were few; most were tied to South Africa, Egypt or
Nigeria, and the lessons say so plainly.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **F1 O*NET Software QA Analysts and Testers:** tasks of documenting defects in a tracker,
  designing test plans, scenarios and scripts, regression, negative and usability testing;
  tools include Selenium, Jira, Git, Jenkins.
* **C1 ISTQB Foundation syllabus (topic titles only):** fundamentals and principles; testing
  across the lifecycle and test levels; static testing and reviews; black box, white box and
  experience based techniques; planning, risk, monitoring and defect management; tool support
  and automation. Used as a checklist that the outline is not missing a core area; wording,
  ordering and examples are our own.

## Outline decisions and why

24 nodes in six sections. Unchanged in the lesson wave.

* **Manual first, phone friendly**: test cases, design techniques, exploratory testing and bug
  reports are the most common signals (8 and 6 adverts) and can be practised on a phone.
* **Mobile testing on real phones** is its own node because four adverts name mobile or Android
  testing and local users meet apps on low cost phones with patchy data.
* **API testing** is a full node (four adverts) and is flagged as needing a laptop.
* **Automation is a full section** because seven adverts name it, flagged as laptop only.
  `tests-in-ci` is optional (one advert plus O*NET).
* **Accessibility and usability** is optional: no Ghana advert names accessibility, but O*NET
  lists usability testing and it is a cheap, phone friendly skill.
* **Static testing and reviews** from C1 are not a node; reviewing requirements is folded into
  `agile-and-issue-trackers` (acceptance criteria). Performance and security testing appear in
  three adverts only as a plus, so they are mentioned in summaries rather than given nodes.
* **ISTQB** is named factually in `certificates-and-first-role` because two adverts mention it;
  no endorsement, no price.
