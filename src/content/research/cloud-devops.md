## Who the track is for

Learners who have finished, or are well into, the backend developer or IT support track. The
catalogue (`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`, row 16) found cloud training at AmaliTech,
Generation Ghana, Ghana Digital Centres, IPMC and Moringa, plus AWS, IBM, edX and Codecademy, and
marked it "fair" for our learners: theory works on a phone, labs need a laptop. Adverts seen
are mostly mid level (one to five years); the only explicitly junior Ghana advert found was an
internship (A1). The outline therefore opens with prerequisites and an honest jobs node, and
says where a laptop becomes necessary.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **F1 O*NET Network and Computer Systems Administrators:** tasks on network security,
  backups and disaster recovery, troubleshooting; tools include Linux, Bash, Ansible, Docker,
  AWS CloudFormation, monitoring tools.
* **F2 O*NET Computer Systems Engineers/Architects:** tasks on stability, security and
  scalability, monitoring, patches, documentation; tools include Terraform, Git, GitLab, Jenkins.
* **C1 AZ-900 skills measured:** cloud concepts (service types, shared responsibility, pricing,
  serverless), architecture (regions, zones, compute, networking, storage, identity, access),
  management (cost, governance, infrastructure as code, monitoring). Read for which topics an
  entry cloud exam covers; ordering and wording are our own.

## Provider facts verified on 28 October 2026

Checked on the official pages listed as V1 to V11. Learner copy describes how the offers work
rather than quoting amounts, and tells learners to check the provider page because terms change.

* **AWS (V1, V2):** at sign up you choose a Free account plan or a Paid account plan. New
  customers receive USD 100 in credits and can earn up to USD 100 more. The Free plan lasts up to
  six months or until credits are used, whichever comes first; no charges unless you upgrade;
  some costly services are excluded. When it ends the account is suspended, data is kept for 90
  days, then erased unless upgraded. Over 30 services are always free within monthly limits.
  FAQ: "a payment method isn't required to sign up for most new customers", but AWS may ask for
  one to verify identity. Lessons say: if asked for card details, stop.
* **Azure (V3):** free account gives USD 200 credit for 30 days plus 12 months of selected free
  services and always free services; needs a phone number and a credit or non prepaid debit
  card (temporary authorisation hold, not a charge). Azure for Students: USD 100 credit with an
  academic email, no credit card. Lessons treat the standard Azure free account as card
  required and do not use it for practice.
* **Google Cloud (V4):** Free Trial gives USD 300 credit over 90 days and requires a credit card
  or other payment method; it does not convert to paid automatically. The Free Tier needs a
  billing account. Lessons treat both as card required.
* **Google Cloud Shell (V5):** "free for users with a Google Cloud account"; default weekly
  quota 50 hours; sessions capped at 12 hours; home directory deleted after 120 days without
  use. The pages do not say whether a billing account is needed, so lessons name it as an
  example with the instruction to stop if asked for payment details.
* **GitHub (V6):** Codespaces free quota for personal accounts (120 compute hours and 15 GB month
  of storage per month; larger machines use the hours faster, and storage counts while a codespace
  exists, even when stopped); without a payment method usage is blocked, not billed. Actions are free
  on public repositories with standard runners. Quickstart uses `actions/checkout@v6` (V11).
* **Killercoda (V7):** free membership, every free scenario usable as much as you like, each
  environment lasts up to one hour then is deleted, no daily or monthly limit, free
  registration needed for scenarios.
* **Not recommended:** Play with Docker shows a notice that it is unavailable from 1 March 2026
  (V10). Community posts on Microsoft Q&A in 2026 say Microsoft Learn's free Azure sandboxes have
  been retired; not verified on an official page, so lessons simply do not rely on them.
* **Certifications (C2, V8, V9):** "Microsoft Certified: Azure Fundamentals" (exam AZ-900,
  beginner level, 45 minutes, free practice assessment on Microsoft Learn); "AWS Certified Cloud
  Practitioner" (for people new to cloud, up to six months of AWS exposure suggested but not
  required, valid three years); Google Cloud "Cloud Digital Leader" (no prerequisites, valid
  three years). No prices recorded or used.

## Outline decisions and why

25 nodes in six sections.

* **Prerequisites first** (`what-you-need-first`) because the brief and catalogue place this
  track after backend and IT support, and every advert except A1 and A12 asks for experience.
* **Linux and networking as a section** even though only three adverts name Linux, because
  O*NET F1 and F2, A3's networking list and the AZ-900 networking objectives all depend on them.
* **Cloud costs is a full node** (`cloud-costs`) with no direct advert signal: A2 mentions cost
  efficient design and C1 covers cost management, and for a learner paying with a cedi card a
  surprise bill is a real risk. Summaries avoid any figures.
* **Kubernetes is optional**: it appears in six adverts but all are mid or senior level.
* **Certifications node** names AWS and Azure fundamentals exams factually because A2 and A3 ask
  for certification; no endorsement and no prices.
* **Named training programmes** (AmaliTech AWS re/Start and others) are not named in learner
  copy, because they were seen only in snippets and third party listings; `first-cloud-role`
  refers generically to free employer run cloud training cohorts.
* **Not included as nodes:** configuration management (Ansible, A10 only), service mesh and
  GitOps (A8 only), ERP and payment card standards (A3 only).
