# Research: Freelancing and Getting Paid track (`freelancing`)

Tier B outline (issue #96). Researched and written 27 October 2026. Outline only: the outline in
`src/content/roadmaps/freelancing.ts` was designed from this research and lessons show as coming
soon. Everything is original. roadmap.sh was not opened, searched or used, and no "<field> roadmap"

## Who the track is for

Freelancing is not an occupation but a way of selling a skill, so this is a cross track guide.
A learner arrives with a skill from another SydCrest track (graphic design, social media, content
creation, data, virtual assistance, web) and needs the business side: choosing a service, proof
of skill, finding clients locally and remotely, proposals, pricing in cedis, simple contracts,
getting paid from Ghana, avoiding scams and building a reputation. The catalogue
(`Planning/12_TRACK_CATALOGUE.md`, row 20) found this only at ALX (Freelancer Academy) among the
platforms surveyed and called it the missing bridge to the "earning" step for several tracks.
The digital marketer track already has one `freelancing` node and the content creation track has
`working-with-brands` and `earning-routes`; this track goes deeper and is shared by all tracks.

## Frameworks and curricula

* **ALX Freelancer Academy (F1):** three modules (from skill to freelance business; attracting
  clients; from first gig to steady income). Skills listed: identifying strengths, niche
  selection, personal branding and digital presence, professional profile and portfolio,
  authority in a niche, client outreach, discovery calls, closing, onboarding, contracts,
  invoicing, delivery, follow up, client management. Used only to confirm topic areas; no text
  or ordering taken. The page states six weeks; a testimonial on the same page says one month.
* **O*NET cross functional skills (F2):** negotiation, persuasion, service orientation, time
  management and management of financial resources map to proposals, pricing, client care and
  project management nodes.
* **Ghana official sources (D1 to D10):** GRA confirms self employed people and consultants
  register and that the Ghana Card PIN is the TIN; Bank of Ghana regulates inward remittance and
  publishes a list of approved payment institutions; the Cyber Security Authority has issued job
  scam alerts in 2023 and September 2026 describing task scams, deposit demands, cloned sites and
  fake fees.
* **ESCO:** not consulted (has been unavailable for other batches; O*NET covered the skill side).

## Outline decisions and why

30 nodes in seven sections (the maximum the tests allow).

* **Safety comes straight after finding clients**, before proposals, because beginners meet
  scams at the searching stage (D7, D8: unsolicited offers, pay to work, task deposits).
  `protecting-accounts` is separate because account renting and identity documents are a
  freelancer specific risk, and marketplace, email and payment accounts are the business.
* **Payments are provider neutral.** `paid-from-abroad` names the three kinds of route
  (marketplace withdrawal, licensed payment or remittance service, bank account for foreign
  transfers) and sends learners to the Bank of Ghana approved list (D5) because the rules changed
  in 2025 and 2026 (D4, D6). No company is named or recommended.
* **Pricing in cedis gets its own node** because every posting states a price model and A9 asks
  for a per item rate; it covers costs such as data and power and the effect of fees and
  exchange rates. No figures are given.
* **Tax and registration are one node** (`records-and-tax`) using only what the GRA pages say:
  who registers, Ghana Card PIN, Taxpayers Portal. No rates or thresholds, which change.
* **The toolkit node says where a laptop is truly needed** (client video editing, web
  development) so the track stays honest about phone limits.
* **`growing-income` is optional**; it matters once a learner has steady clients.
* Overlap: the digital marketer `freelancing` node and content creation earning nodes stay as
  short pointers; this track is the shared deep version. Mentoring cohorts are not mentioned.

## Lesson wave (issue #198, 28 October 2026)

All 30 nodes now have original lessons in
`src/content/roadmaps/content/freelancing.content.ts`. No nodes were added, removed, merged or
renamed. The current official pages were read before writing the money and tax lessons.

* **paid-from-abroad** states only what D13 says (registration of foreign operators with the
  Bank of Ghana, banks and PSPs only as agents, settlement in cedis, payout channels, no extra
  fees on the receiver). It names no company. Because D5 has no remittance category and D14 is
  not a full list, the lesson tells learners to check the Ghanaian payout side on D5, search the
  Bank of Ghana site for its latest notices on money transfer operators (D15), and ask their bank
  or wallet provider. Because D13 describes these services as primarily person to person, the
  lesson tells learners to ask a service whether it accepts payments for work. Foreign currency
  account rules were not verified, so the lesson only says to ask your bank.
* **records-and-tax** states only what D16 and D17 say. No thresholds, rates, deadlines or
  instalment rules. Business name registration is described without claiming it can be done
  online (D18).
* **spotting-scams** uses the CSA pattern and reporting channels from D7 (292 call or text,
  WhatsApp 0501603111, report@csa.gov.gh); case counts and amounts are left out.
* **pricing-in-cedis** uses one clearly labelled made up calculation to show the method; no
  market rates or income claims anywhere in the track. No lesson suggests SydCrest handles,
  holds or settles money.
