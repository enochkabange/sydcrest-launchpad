## 2. Employer costs in Ghana (employees)

### 2.1 SSNIT and pensions (three-tier scheme)
- **Total 18.5% of basic salary: 13% employer plus 5.5% employee.** Of this, **13.5% goes to SSNIT (Tier 1)** and **5% to a private Tier 2 trustee** licensed by the NPRA **[S]** (TaxLawGH, GroConsult; consistent across EOR guides). SSNIT's own rate page did not load **[U on primary]**.
- Within Tier 1's 13.5%, 2.5% goes to NHIS **[S]** (Databank). Tier 3 is voluntary.
- **2026 insurable earnings, from 1 Jan 2026:** maximum **GHS 69,000 per month** (up from 61,000), giving a maximum Tier 1 contribution of **GHS 9,315**. Minimum **GHS 587.80**, giving a minimum contribution of **GHS 79.40** **[V]** (SSNIT public notice dated 13 Jan 2026).
- Deel's page shows 13.5% employer social security, not 13% **[V]**, which conflicts with the 13% that other sources give. The difference is likely how the Tier 1 and Tier 2 split is presented **[U]**.
- The employee's 5.5% is deducted before PAYE is calculated **[S]** (GroConsult).

### 2.2 PAYE: Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 1178), effective 1 Sep 2026 [V, GRA]
Monthly chargeable income, resident individuals:

| Band | GHS | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| First | 588 | 0% |
| Next | 80 | 5% |
| Next | 100 | 10% |
| Next | 2,900 | 17.5% |
| Next | 16,000 | 25% |

| Exceeding | 50,000 | 35% |

Annual equivalents: first 7,056 at 0%, then 960 / 1,200 / 34,800 / 192,000 / 363,984, with income over 600,000 at 35%. Non-residents pay a flat 25% **[S]**. Employers file and remit PAYE by the **15th of the following month** **[S]**.
- **Conflict:** GroConsult and Powersoft show different band widths (110 / 130 / 3,000). Use the **GRA table** above. GRA's general PAYE page still shows the outdated 2020 bands, so don't cite it.
- Bonuses: a concessionary 5% rate on bonuses up to 15% of annual basic salary is reported by one source only **[U]**.

### 2.3 Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651) basics [V, primary text]
- **Written contract** required for employment of 6 months or more (s.12). A written statement of particulars is due within 2 months of starting (s.13).
- **Notice** (s.17): **1 month** for contracts of 3 years or more, **2 weeks** for contracts under 3 years, 7 days for week-to-week contracts. Notice must be in writing. Pay in lieu is allowed (s.18(4)).
- **Annual leave:** at least **15 working days** with full pay per calendar year of continuous service (s.20). Certified sick leave does not count as annual leave (s.24). An agreement to give up leave is void (s.31).
- **Maternity leave:** at least **12 weeks** on full pay, plus 2 weeks for abnormal or multiple births (s.57). Paternity leave is not in the 2003 Act.
- Redundancy compensation: s.65 (amount is negotiated, not fixed by statute) **[S]**.
- **Minimum wage 2026:** GHS **21.77 per day**, effective 1 Jan 2026 (up 9% from 19.97) **[S]** (Ghanaian Times, WageIndicator). Deel's page still shows GHS 490.05 per month and Remote's shows GHS 18.15 per day. Both are stale.
- Standard hours: 8 per day, 40 per week **[S]** (Deel).

### 2.4 13th month
**Not required by law.** It is discretionary, set by company policy, the contract or a collective agreement **[S]** (several EOR guides, e.g. RemoFirst and Asanify). Some guides say it is common among formal-sector employers **[U]**.

### 2.5 Worked example: USD 1,000 per month, all basic salary
FX assumption: **USD 1 = GHS 11.65** (interbank about 11.64–11.67, 2–9 Nov 2026) **[S]** (GNA, ModernGhana). So gross salary = **GHS 11,650**.

| Line | GHS | ≈ USD |
|---|---|---|
| Gross basic salary | 11,650.00 | 1,000 |
| Employee SSNIT 5.5% | −640.75 | −55 |
| Chargeable income | 11,009.25 | |
| PAYE (Act 1178 bands): 0 + 4.00 + 10.00 + 507.50 + 1,835.31 | −2,356.81 | −202 |
| **Net take-home pay** | **8,652.44** | **≈ 743** |
| Employer SSNIT 13% | +1,514.50 | +130 |

Not included above: leave cover, any 13th month (about +8.3% if paid), equipment, health insurance top-up, the EOR deposit (often one month), and FX spread. The salary is below the GHS 69,000 SSNIT ceiling, so the cap does not apply.

## 3. Contractor route

**The freelancer's own obligations (Ghana-resident individual):**
- Fees are **business income**. Work done while in Ghana is Ghana-source income even if the client and the paying account are abroad. Residents are also taxed on foreign-source business income, with foreign tax credits available. There is no special flat rate for online income **[S]** (TaxLawGH freelancer guide).
- Register with the **Ghana Card PIN as the TIN** **[S]**.
- **Instalment (provisional) tax:** file an estimate and pay quarterly on 31 Mar, 30 Jun, 30 Sep and 31 Dec. The **annual return is due by 30 Apr** **[S]**. Keep records for 6 years (Act 915) **[S]**.
- Small traders: the Modified Taxation Scheme charges a presumptive **3% of turnover** for individuals turning over between GHS 20,000 and 750,000 a year **[V]** (GRA Act 1178 page). Whether this suits professional service freelancers is **[U]**. Check with GRA.
- **VAT:** under the VAT Act, 2025 (Act 1151), from 1 Jan 2026, **service providers have no registration threshold**: they must register within 30 days of starting a taxable activity **[S]** (several advisory firms). The GHS 750,000 threshold applies only to goods. A foreign client does not automatically make the service zero-rated **[S]**. **[U]**: how GRA treats a small freelancer exporting services. This is a real compliance gap for workers.

**Foreign company obligations:**
- A UK or US client paying a non-Ghanaian-entity invoice has **no Ghana withholding duty that we could verify**. The 7.5% WHT on services applies to Ghana-resident payers **[S]** (Teamed). Permanent establishment risk from having one remote contractor was **not addressed by any source found [U]**. It is generally low for a single contractor but rises if the person habitually concludes contracts for the company.
- If a remote worker is in fact an **employee of a foreign employer**, the income is employment income. TaxLawGH says the parties must "determine how Ghana payroll withholding, registration and filing obligations are to be met" **[S]**. This is exactly the gap an EOR fills.

**Misclassification risk:**
- Ghana looks at the **substance** of the relationship (control, integration, dominant purpose, mutuality of obligation), not the contract label **[S]** (UPSA law journal, Teamed). Labour Act s.12 already presumes a written employment contract for engagements of 6 months or more **[V]**.
- Exposure falls on the engaging company: GRA reassessment going back **6 years**, or with no limit for fraud or wilful default (Revenue Administration Act 2016 s.37). SSNIT penalty of **3% per month** on unpaid contributions (National Pensions Act 2008, Act 766, s.64). Possible criminal liability under Act 766 s.3(10) **[S]** (Teamed, vendor content; check against the Act text).
- Moving someone to an EOR later **does not cure past misclassification** **[S]** (Playroll, Teamed).
- In practice, enforcement against foreign companies with no Ghana presence is **untested in sources found [U]**. The worker, however, loses SSNIT, leave and notice protection.

## 5. Data protection: Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843)

**Registration (this applies to SydCrest directly as a Ghana-facing controller):**
- Every data controller that intends to process personal data must register with the Data Protection Commission (s.27(1), quoted on the DPC FAQ) **[V]**. New controllers should register **within 20 days of starting business**. Registration is **renewed every 2 years** **[S]** (dataprotection.africa factsheet, DLA Piper). Failing to register can bring a fine of up to 250 penalty units and/or up to 2 years in prison **[S]**.
- **Fees** by category (DPC FAQ on the new dpc.gov.gh domain): **Small businesses and startups GHS 156**, Medium GHS 1,170, Large GHS 2,340, Specialised industries GHS 5,000 **[V]**. An older page on dataprotection.org.gh showed GHS 120 and GHS 900, now superseded. The full schedule (DPC-NEW-FEES.pdf, Feb 2026) is image-only, so we could not extract the category definitions or renewal fees **[U]**.
- The DPC's old domain dataprotection.org.gh now points to **dpc.gov.gh**. Registration is online at app.dpc.gov.gh **[V]**.
- Large controllers (turnover of GHS 5m or more, or 250+ staff, or certain sectors) need a certified data protection supervisor. Others may appoint one **[S]** (DLA Piper).
- The DPC is enforcing actively: 2,575 controllers registered in 2026, and expired certificates were flagged in Oct 2026 inspections **[S]** (GNA, Oct 2026).

**Transferring personal data abroad:**
- **Act 843 has no specific cross-border transfer regime**: no adequacy test, no approval and no standard clauses **[S]** (DLA Piper, dataprotection.africa).
- Related rules: the Act applies where the controller is established in Ghana, **or where the data originates partly or wholly from Ghana** (s.45, section number per secondary sources). Data that only transits Ghana is excluded (s.45(4)) **[S]**. Processors outside Ghana, such as cloud hosts, must comply with Ghana's rules (s.30) **[S]** (Chambers). Personal data about foreign data subjects that is sent into Ghana must be processed under the law of the originating country (s.18(2)) **[S]**.
- DPC guidance: do not host data in countries that do not regulate personal data processing, and before sharing with a third party, check it is registered and complies with the 8 principles **[V]** (DPC FAQ).
- Selling personal data is an offence: up to 2,500 penalty units and/or 5 years in prison **[S]**. SydCrest must never "sell" candidate profiles. Sharing must be consent-based and limited to a stated purpose.

**Profiling and automated decisions (s.41):**
- A data subject can give written notice requiring that **no decision significantly affecting them is based solely on automated processing**. Where such a decision is made, the controller must **promptly notify** the person and tell them of their right to ask for **reconsideration** **[S]** (B&P Associates via Legal500, 6 Jun 2025; dataprotection.africa). The exact time limits in s.41 are **[U]** because we could not access the primary text (GhaLII blocked).
- This matters for SydCrest: any automated candidate scoring, ranking or shortlisting shown to employers needs a human in the loop, a notice to candidates and a reconsideration route.
- **Reform:** the government announced a **new Data Protection Bill** in March 2026, covering AI, automated decision-making and cross-border flows. A 2025 draft would repeal Act 843, but it is **not law** **[S]**. Watch this.

## 6. Implications for SydCrest

**Economics.** At a USD 1,000 salary, an employee hired through an EOR costs a UK, EU or US employer about **USD 1,590–1,830 a month**. Statutory costs add about 13–14%, and the EOR fee adds USD 459–699. The fee hurts most at junior salaries. The contractor route costs about USD 1,000 plus USD 0–49 in platform fees, but pushes tax, VAT, FX and misclassification risk onto the worker and the employer. Expect most early employers to start with contractors. SydCrest should make the EOR path easy for anything ongoing and full-time beyond about 6 months.

**Partners to approach (in order):**
1. **Deel**: Ghana EOR verified on its own page, published USD 599 and USD 49 pricing, and widely known to UK and US buyers. Ask about a referral or partner programme.
2. **Multiplier**: lowest published global fee (USD 459–499) and possibly its own Ghana entity. Verify both.
3. **One Ghana-based or Africa-focused EOR** (Workpay, or a local firm such as GroConsult) for cheaper quote-based pricing at junior salary levels. Due diligence: SSNIT and GRA registration, NPRA Tier 2 trustee, references.
4. **Remote**: only once it confirms Ghana EOR is live.
5. Payments: **Grey** or **Payoneer** for contractor receiving accounts. **Wise Business** for employers paying GHS straight to bank or MoMo (wallet cap GHS 2,800 per transfer).

Drop for now: Rippling, Papaya, Eden/Onafriq and Bunce. None has verified Ghana EOR coverage.

**One-page "Hiring in Ghana" guide for employers. Include:**
- Two routes side by side: **EOR (recommended for ongoing full-time roles)** and **contractor (short projects only)**, with the all-in cost example above.
- Statutory basics: SSNIT 13% employer + 5.5% employee; PAYE under Act 1178 (0–35%, effective 1 Sep 2026); 15 days' annual leave; 12 weeks' maternity leave; notice of 2 weeks (contracts under 3 years) or 1 month; no mandatory 13th month; minimum wage GHS 21.77 per day.
- Misclassification red flags: fixed hours, manager control, 6+ months, a single client. Converting to an EOR later doesn't erase past exposure.
